# Branding your fork

**AIDE Framework** is the project name. Alpha Health is the originating publisher, credited in the footer. The framework's roles, records, and workflows are independent of any company or industry.

An adopting organization can use its own name, logo, colors, and terminology. It supplies its own private context, identities, policies, and GitHub workspace. Neither Alpha Health products nor a shared Alpha Health account are dependencies.

## The upstream visual style

The design uses black and white surfaces, bold typography, generous spacing, and a restrained red ribbon. The origin credit stays secondary to the project and its purpose.

| Element | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background | `#ffffff` | `#101113` |
| Main text | `#101113` | `#f7f7f8` |
| Secondary text | `#666970` | `#a3a4ab` |
| Accent | `#f5224e` | `#f5224e` |

The SVG assets contain editable text and vector shapes. The README selects the light or dark image through a `picture` element and includes a default image for other viewers.

## Adapt the identity

1. Change the README title and introductory copy to fit your project.
2. Replace or edit the cover and delivery graphics in `assets/`, including both theme variants and their alternative text.
3. Update links to point to your fork where appropriate. Keep the upstream reference where it helps explain provenance.
4. Replace deployment placeholders in your private workspace. Keep company names, accounts, and business-specific assumptions out of reusable examples.
5. Preserve the existing MIT copyright and license notice when copying the project. Visual branding is separate from that notice.

Use attribution to identify the source of the work, not to imply that Alpha Health operates or endorses your deployment.
